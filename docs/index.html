1. Abstract

This study tests whether cross-asset futures-options implied volatility and skew contain useful information about future S&P 500 drawdown regimes. The empirical design combines a long-history Nasdaq Data Link AR/IVM options panel, daily equity and volatility benchmarks, and a short intraday ES case study. The core signal is an interpretable stress score built from equity implied volatility, broad cross-asset implied volatility, dispersion, downside-skew pressure, and PCA concentration.

The evidence is strongest for risk monitoring and exposure control. Equity-index ATM implied volatility and ES downside-skew pressure materially raise forward SPX drawdown event rates. VIX term structure remains the stronger direct probability benchmark in the 2020+ holdout, but the AR/IVM stress layer improves the path behavior of a simple shifted-signal protection overlay. The strongest AR/IVM sensitivity result is an extreme-stress cash rule; it is reported separately from the reference overlay because its threshold and exposure setting require a locked follow-up test.

2. Research Question

The practical question is whether a risk manager can ignore much of the ordinary daily and intraday noise and focus on market states where equity moves are more likely to be large, persistent, and cross-asset in nature. The proposed answer is a point-in-time stress monitor that conditions on large equity moves and cross-asset option-implied variables.

This is an extension of the original cross-asset correlation idea. The paper measures co-movement in both realized futures returns and option-implied stress variables. Realized returns show how asset prices moved together; option-implied volatility and skew show how derivatives markets priced uncertainty, downside demand, and convexity across asset classes at the same point in time.

3. Literature Position

The study sits next to established work on option-implied tail risk, changing correlations in stress periods, PCA concentration, dynamic conditional correlation, and spillover networks. Cboe's VIX and SKEW methodologies motivate option-implied volatility and skew as market-based measures of expected volatility and tail risk. Bollerslev, Tauchen, and Zhou (2009) and Kelly and Jiang (2014) connect option-implied and cross-sectional tail-risk measures to asset prices. Longin and Solnik (2001), Forbes and Rigobon (2002), Engle (2002), Diebold and Yilmaz (2012), and Kritzman, Li, Page, and Rigobon (2011) provide the benchmark frame for stress co-movement, dynamic dependence, spillovers, and PCA absorption.

Although PCA, implied volatility, and conditional correlation are all established tools, this investigation brings them together around a narrower question: whether cross-asset option-implied co-movement can serve as a practical stress layer for equity drawdown protection. The result offers a market-based perspective on when equity risk is becoming more broadly priced across rates, FX, commodities, and volatility markets.

4. Data

The main empirical layer is the AR/IVM futures-options panel. It provides daily implied-volatility model fields across equity indices, rates, FX, energy, metals, and agriculture. The daily benchmark workbook anchors SPX total return, VIX term structure, rates, credit, FX, and commodity variables. The intraday workbook is intentionally treated as a case study because the available desktop history is short.

The daily AR/IVM panel is sufficient for the daily stress-regime evidence because it spans the global financial crisis, euro-area stress, Covid, the 2022 inflation/rates shock, and more recent regimes. The intraday evidence is useful for method design, not final regime validation.

5. Signal Construction

The stress score is a transparent composite. Components are shifted one day, standardized with rolling information, and averaged so the score can be explained as a set of market conditions rather than an opaque fitted rule.

6. Cross-Asset Correlations

The cross-asset exercise asks whether large ES or SPX stress states are associated with a recognizable macro basket. Post-2018 AR/IVM shocks show very high co-movement between ES, NQ, and RTY implied volatility, with secondary links to rates, yen, gold, FX, energy, metals, and agriculture. Futures returns recover a different risk-on/risk-off structure: equity beta clusters together, while dollar strength, duration, and yen often sit on the defensive side.

7. Event-Rate Tests

Before fitting return or exposure rules, the analysis tests whether stress indicators lift future drawdown event rates relative to unconditional base rates. This is a useful first filter because it answers whether the signal is aligned with the target event at all.

8. Forecasting Benchmarks

The direct forecasting test uses a chronological 2020+ holdout. Brier score measures squared probability forecast error, so lower is better. Average precision is useful for rare events because it evaluates how well high-risk dates are ranked. In this run, VIX term structure remains the strongest direct forecasting benchmark.

9. Portfolio Overlay

The overlay applies shifted stress signals to SPX total return exposure. In every rule, the stress signal is observed first, the position is changed the next trading day, and a 1 bp turnover cost is charged. The portfolio never shorts the market and never trades options directly. The test is intentionally simple: if the stress layer has useful information, it should improve path quality before any complex hedge construction is added.

The table shows both the main benchmark rules and the best AR/IVM sensitivity rule. The AR/IVM reference overlay improves Sharpe from 0.75 to 0.89 and reduces maximum drawdown from -33.8% to -24.9%, while giving up less than one percentage point of annual return versus buy-and-hold.

The 95/cash AR/IVM row is the best-performing action rule in this particular 2020+ grid. It is still the same risk layer. The difference is that it waits for a more extreme stress threshold and then exits equity exposure fully for the next day. That result is promising, but it should be treated as a candidate rule to lock and retest rather than as the main selected strategy.

10. PCA and Intraday Evidence

PCA is useful here as a compression and concentration tool. It helps measure whether cross-asset stress is becoming dominated by a small number of common factors. It does not provide causal attribution without additional identification work.

11. Discussion

The empirical results support the use of cross-asset option-implied stress as a drawdown-risk monitoring layer. The strongest evidence comes from direct stress measurement: equity-index implied volatility, ES downside skew, broad cross-asset volatility, and PCA concentration identify periods with higher forward SPX drawdown rates. The signal is less persuasive as a standalone daily probability forecast, where VIX term structure remains the stronger benchmark.

The options panel is not a detour from cross-asset correlation. It is a forward-looking version of the same co-movement question: when ES stress rises, do other asset classes also reprice volatility and skew in a way that signals a broader regime shift?

The portfolio result follows naturally from that evidence. The reference AR/IVM overlay does not attempt to forecast every down day. It reduces exposure only in high-stress states and is evaluated as a left-tail protection rule. The central economic trade-off is lower volatility and smaller drawdowns in exchange for some opportunity cost during market rebounds.

The main external-validity questions are implementation and attribution questions. The relevant tests are clean continuous-futures return data, investable hedge instruments, richer dependence models, and longer intraday history.

12. Conclusion

Cross-asset option-implied data is a credible stress-regime layer for equity drawdown protection. It complements realized returns because it measures the market price of uncertainty, downside skew, and convexity demand before realized outcomes are fully known.

The empirical relationships are consistent across the paper. ES implied-volatility shocks co-move most with NQ and RTY, but also pick up rates, yen, gold, FX, and selected commodity stress. Equity-index ATM implied volatility and ES downside-skew pressure raise forward SPX drawdown event rates. PCA absorption is useful as a stress-concentration diagnostic, while VIX term structure remains the tougher direct forecasting benchmark.

The useful application is not pure return forecasting. The evidence supports an auditable protection layer that connects realized co-movement, option-implied co-movement, PCA concentration, point-in-time validation, and portfolio drawdown control.
