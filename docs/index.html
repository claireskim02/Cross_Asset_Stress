ChronoSwan Research Log

Point-in-time detection and attribution of large ES impulses.

ChronoSwan studies whether large 60-minute moves in ES1 can be decomposed into recurring cross-asset drivers. The current branch benchmarks event-conditioned correlations against PCA, then asks whether any structured signal remains useful for next-bar impulse risk.

Raw Bloomberg/Finaeon files, parquet caches, and executed notebooks are kept local. This public page reports methodology, sanitized sample rows, and high-level local findings without publishing vendor data.

Abstract

The working hypothesis is that most intraday market moves are noisy, but the largest ES1 impulses are more structured. The research design defines a large move using a shifted rolling threshold known before the bar, then compares pairwise driver correlations with a PCA factor decomposition. PCA is useful only if it adds factor concentration or driver structure beyond the conditional-correlation benchmark.

This is not a black-swan prediction exercise. It is an attribution and stress-detection workflow that can later support an LLM agent for disciplined synthesis of market-driver evidence.

Method

Event Definition

A large ES impulse is the absolute 60-minute ES1 log return exceeding the shifted 95th percentile of absolute ES1 returns in the prior rolling 20-day window. The threshold is shifted by one bar to avoid look-ahead bias.

Benchmarks

The first benchmark is conditional correlation: if correlations on large ES moves already explain the driver story, PCA should be treated as compression rather than discovery. PCA is then fit on standardized cross-asset returns, excluding ES1.

Preliminary Findings

Large ES moves look meaningfully more factor-concentrated than the all-bar sample. This supports the research premise that the important impulses are less random than the full intraday stream.

Literature Position

The underlying angle has precedent. Extreme-dependence studies, contagion/interdependence work, DCC models, spillover networks, and PCA absorption-ratio research all overlap with this problem. The narrower research contribution is the point-in-time ES impulse workflow and its direct benchmark against conditional correlations.

  • Longin and Solnik, extreme market correlations: doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00340
  • Forbes and Rigobon, contagion versus interdependence: nber.org/papers/w7267
  • Engle, dynamic conditional correlation: doi.org/10.1198/073500102288618487
  • Diebold and Yilmaz, spillovers: doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2012.08.006
  • Kritzman, Li, Page, and Rigobon, PCA absorption ratio: doi.org/10.2469/faj.v67.n1.5

Interpretation: the literature makes the novelty boundary clear. The useful contribution is the point-in-time ES impulse workflow, not the generic use of PCA.

Next Research Steps

  • Extend beyond the Bloomberg desktop intraday limit if a deeper licensed feed becomes available.
  • Compare event-conditioned PCA with DCC and spillover-network baselines.
  • Stabilize PCA by testing top-10%, top-20%, and weighted-PCA definitions, since top-5% windows can be sparse.
  • Add timestamp-aware macro/news annotations only after the structured market benchmark is frozen.
  • Use an open-weight local LLM only for driver synthesis and audit trails, not as an unconstrained prediction engine.

Interpretation: the next bottleneck is data depth and validation design. Model complexity should come after the 140-day proof of concept is stress-tested on longer history.
